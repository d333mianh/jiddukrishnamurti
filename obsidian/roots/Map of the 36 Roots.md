## I. Structures of Consciousness

*The machinery of the conditioned mind — how the known is built and sustained.*

1. [[thought|Thought & Knowledge]] — **pilot**  
   <small>Thought is the material response of memory, knowledge, and experience operating through the brain.</small>
2. [[conditioning|Conditioning]]  
   <small>Conditioning is the shaping of the mind by culture, tradition, religion, nationality, education, and accumulated experience, so that response arises from the past rather than from direct perception.</small>
3. [[self|The Self ("the me")]] — **probation pair**  
   <small>The self, the 'me', the ego, is for Krishnamurti a bundle of memories, images, wants, and self-centred activity built by thought, not a permanent entity.</small>
4. [[psychological-time|Psychological Time & Becoming]]  
   <small>Beyond chronological time, Krishnamurti describes psychological time as the mind's movement from 'what is' toward 'what should be' — the becoming, achieving, and postponement by which thought avoids facing the present fact.</small>
5. [[relationship|Relationship & Image-making]]  
   <small>Relationship is the mirror in which the self is revealed.</small>
6. [[division|Division / Fragmentation]]  
   <small>Division is thought's fragmenting action: inner and outer, observer and observed, me and not-me, nationality and belief.</small>
7. [[belief|Belief & Ideals]]  
   <small>Belief, faith, ideology, and dogma are for Krishnamurti forms of psychological security that thought invents and that prevent seeing 'what is'.</small>
8. [[consciousness|Consciousness & Its Content]]  
   <small>Consciousness is its content — the whole field of thought, memory, fear, and conditioning shared by humankind.</small>
9. [[word-naming|The Word & Naming]] — **provisional root**  
   <small>'The word is not the thing.' Krishnamurti investigates how naming, verbal association, symbols, and description substitute for direct perception — how the description is mistaken for the described, and how naming a state (fear, anger) can sustain the very reaction being named.</small>

## II. Human Experience & Relationship

*The felt symptoms that machinery produces in daily life.*

10. [[fear|Fear]] — **pilot**  
    <small>Fear is the psychological response produced when thought projects remembered pain or imagined danger into the future.</small>
11. [[pleasure|Desire & Pleasure]]  
    <small>Krishnamurti traces desire as the movement from perception to sensation to thought's image and pursuit, and pleasure as thought's demand to continue a sensation.</small>
12. [[suffering|Suffering (Sorrow)]]  
    <small>Sorrow, for Krishnamurti, is not only personal grief but the sorrow of humankind, arising from self-concern, attachment, and thought's resistance to loss.</small>
13. [[loneliness|Loneliness & Aloneness]]  
    <small>Loneliness is the ache of self-isolation and inner emptiness that thought and activity try to cover.</small>
14. [[conflict|Conflict]]  
    <small>Conflict is the friction of duality — between what is and what should be, desire and desire, the observer and the observed.</small>
15. [[violence|Violence]]  
    <small>Violence, for Krishnamurti, is far wider than physical harm: it includes anger, comparison, conformity, ambition, and the imitation of an ideal — including the ideal of non-violence.</small>
16. [[comparison|Comparison & Measurement]]  
    <small>Psychological comparison — measuring oneself against another, against an ideal, against yesterday — is for Krishnamurti a root of becoming, envy, competition, conformity, and the sense of insufficiency; 'all comparison is violence.' Measurement is the mind's instrument, and the immeasurable is precisely what lies beyond it.</small>
17. [[attachment|Security & Attachment]]  
    <small>The craving for psychological security drives attachment — to people, beliefs, possessions, and self-images — and Krishnamurti shows that attachment breeds fear, dependence, jealousy, and sorrow.</small>
18. [[authority|Authority & Following]]  
    <small>Krishnamurti rejects spiritual and psychological authority — gurus, saviours, systems, and the authority of one's own accumulated experience — as incompatible with the discovery of truth.</small>

## III. Observation, Inquiry & Action

*The turning — seeing without the observer, and acting from that seeing.*

19. [[freedom|Freedom]] — **pilot**  
    <small>Freedom is not choice, permissiveness, or movement toward an ideal; it is freedom from psychological conditioning, authority, fear, and the known.</small>
20. [[observer-observed|The Observer Is the Observed]]  
    <small>Krishnamurti's central perceptual discovery: the thinker is the thought, the experiencer the experience, the observer the observed.</small>
21. [[awareness|Awareness & Attention]]  
    <small>Awareness is choiceless observation — inward and outward — without judgement, condemnation, or the effort of concentration.</small>
22. [[insight|Insight & Intelligence]]  
    <small>Insight is direct, immediate perception of truth that is not the conclusion of thought, memory, or analysis; Krishnamurti holds that seeing the false as false is itself the action that frees.</small>
23. [[learning|Learning & Education]]  
    <small>Learning, for Krishnamurti, is a living movement of observing and understanding, not the accumulation of knowledge to be stored and repeated.</small>
24. [[truth|Truth & "What Is"]]  
    <small>Krishnamurti holds that truth is a pathless land, to be discovered directly and never through belief, authority, or method — it is not produced by thought.</small>
25. [[order|Order & Disorder]]  
    <small>Order, for Krishnamurti, is not imposed discipline, conformity, or a blueprint, but the natural outcome of understanding disorder in oneself.</small>
26. [[action|Action]]  
    <small>Krishnamurti examines action that springs from an idea, ideal, or conclusion — always incomplete and productive of conflict — versus action that is the immediate expression of perception, where the seeing and the doing are one.</small>
27. [[self-knowledge|Self-knowledge]] — **probation pair**  
    <small>Self-knowledge is for Krishnamurti the foundation of all understanding: the ceaseless observation of oneself in the mirror of relationship, moment to moment, without accumulating a conclusion about oneself.</small>

## IV. Ending, Transformation & the Sacred

*What is not put together by thought — the immeasurable, not a destination.*

28. [[love|Love & Compassion]]  
    <small>Krishnamurti approaches love by negation — it is not desire, pleasure, attachment, jealousy, dependence, or the image thought builds.</small>
29. [[death|Death & Ending]]  
    <small>Krishnamurti treats death not as a distant event to fear but as ending — the dying each day to memory, attachment, and the known.</small>
30. [[meditation|Meditation & Silence]]  
    <small>For Krishnamurti meditation is not a method, practice, mantra, or system — all of which condition the mind — but the emptying of consciousness of the known, out of which comes a silence not induced by thought.</small>
31. [[beauty|Beauty]]  
    <small>Beauty, for Krishnamurti, is not merely in the object, the picture, or the poem but in a state of mind free of the self — where, in the presence of a mountain or great beauty, the 'me' is momentarily absent.</small>
32. [[nature|Nature & the Earth]]  
    <small>Krishnamurti's relationship with nature — the mountains, trees, rivers, light, and living things — pervades his talks and writings as a doorway to sensitivity and the absence of the self.</small>
33. [[energy|Energy & Passion]]  
    <small>Krishnamurti speaks of the energy dissipated in conflict, fear, and the self, and of the undivided energy released when those wastages end — an energy needed for transformation and for meditation.</small>
34. [[psychological-revolution|Transformation & Mutation]]  
    <small>Krishnamurti calls for a radical transformation — total and immediate, not the gradual reform of becoming — that alone can end humanity's inherited disorder.</small>
35. [[religious-mind|The Religious Mind]] — **probation pair**  
    <small>The religious mind, for Krishnamurti, has nothing to do with organized religion, belief, ritual, or authority.</small>
36. [[sacred|The Sacred / The Immeasurable]] — **probation pair**  
    <small>Krishnamurti points, always by negation, to something sacred, nameless, and immeasurable that may be when the mind is empty of the self and of thought — 'that benediction', 'the otherness', the timeless.</small>
